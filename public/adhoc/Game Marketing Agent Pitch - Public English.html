Problem
2 / 10

The problem is not that teams ignore marketing. The problem is that few teams can keep doing it well.

1

No dedicated operator

Small teams rarely have a dedicated marketing or community person.

2

Hard prioritization

Generic advice is easy to find. Good judgment depends on stage, platform, genre, and resources.

3

Scattered context

Chat tools help, but plans, assets, versions, and feedback quickly get fragmented.

4

Execution drag

Calendars, copy, community replies, summaries, and follow-ups consume real time.

Not This
3 / 10

This is not an “auto-posting bot.”

For this category, safety boundaries and human approval matter more than full automation.

×

Not pretending to be human

The agent should not impersonate players or community members.

×

Not deciding budget

Money, platform settings, and external commitments require approval.

×

Not a one-off prompt

The value is persistent project memory and operating rhythm.

×

Not a 20-hour expert interview

Experts should calibrate, review, and provide test cases.

×

Not a frozen clone

The agent should keep learning from each project and team.

What It Does
4 / 10

We want a long-running marketing operator, not just another chat window.

It helps teams move these workflows

  • Project intake and stage diagnosis
  • Comparable game research
  • Launch, post-launch, and campaign planning
  • Content calendar and platform-specific copy
  • Community signal and feedback summaries
  • Asset and version tracking
  • Review queue and human approval

It helps humans decide faster

The early product should research, draft, organize, remind, and queue decisions. The goal is to reduce cognitive load and operational drag, not to remove human judgment.

Method
5 / 10

Method: baseline first, expert calibration second

Start with public knowledge. Use expert time for the highest-value work: test cases, critique, scoring, and correction.

Step 1

→

Knowledge Stack
6 / 10

Three knowledge layers: from general ability to team-specific judgment

Public Baseline

Public resources, platform docs, industry guides, interview questions, and case libraries. Goal: act like a competent junior or mid-level marketing operator.

Expert-Calibrated Layer

Seed questions, model answers, critique, scoring, and corrections from experienced people. Goal: move beyond generic advice.

Customer-Specific Layer

Fetched game context, project history, team resources, campaign history, voice preferences, usage habits, and approve/reject records. Goal: become more useful to each team every week.

Adaptive Learning
7 / 10

After delivery, it keeps learning from the actual game, team, and market loop.

This is the compounding advantage: the agent is not a static playbook. It fetches project context, observes how people use it, and turns every review, campaign, and feedback signal into better operating memory.

Learns the game

From live project context

Store pages, builds, patch notes, trailers, screenshots, characters, competitors, comments, and asset libraries become working context.

Learns the team

From real usage patterns

It notices what the team accepts, rewrites, rejects, escalates, or repeatedly asks for — then adapts tone, cadence, and format.

Learns the workflow

From operating rhythm

It fits around review queues, launch milestones, content calendars, approval habits, platform priorities, and real resource constraints.

Learns what works

From market feedback

Campaign results, comments, creator feedback, CTR/CVR snapshots, and post-mortems become better policy and recommendations.

Expert Time
8 / 10

The first ask is small: high-value seed inputs, not a long interview.

1

React to the direction

Who would this help? Where is the real value?

2

Give 3-5 test cases

What would you ask to tell whether someone understands game marketing?

3

Share answer criteria

What should a good answer mention? What are obvious red flags?

Then we use those cases to generate agent answers and bring them back for review.

Calibration Loop
9 / 10

Each calibration round should make improvement visible.

What the reviewer sees

  • 3-5 agent case answers
  • Where answers are generic, junior, or wrong
  • Missing constraints and priorities
  • Which decisions require human approval

What we update afterward

  • Policy
  • Scoring rubric
  • Playbook and checklists
  • Golden cases
  • Before / after comparisons
What We Need
10 / 10

What would help at this stage

We build the baseline first, then bring back the agent’s answers for review.

01

A few test cases

What questions or scenarios reveal whether someone actually understands game marketing or operations?

02

A later review session

Review the agent’s answers, point out where they sound generic, and help turn the critique into the next version.

Principle: external actions stay behind human approval. Early value comes from planning, research, drafting, organization, and calibration.
